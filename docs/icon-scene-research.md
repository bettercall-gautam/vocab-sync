# Watchlist Writing-Scene Research

This note records the first, deliberately small candidate set for a Vocab Sync icon. Each candidate was selected from the user’s Watchlist because the protagonist or antagonist is visibly writing on paper or using a writing machine in the film.

| Title | Confirmed writing moment | Why it can work as an icon | Initial visual assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Lunchbox** | Ila and Saajan communicate through handwritten notes delivered inside a lunchbox. | The writing is central to the plot and connects directly to a personal vocabulary tool. | Warm, quiet, and meaningful, but the final still should show a hand, pen, and note clearly. |
| **Joker** | Arthur Fleck writes in his journal at a table, including overhead shots of his hand and notebook. | The tight overhead composition makes the hand, pen, and page easy to recognize at small size. | Strongest visual crop, but intentionally dark and unsettling. |
| **The Prestige** | The screenplay explicitly shows Angier opening a leather journal and starting to write, while Borden also writes with paper and pencil. | Fountain-pen and journal imagery fit a literary, vintage icon direction. | Strong period atmosphere, but a clean source still must be found. |
| **Gone Girl** | Amy writes diary entries with multiple pens, including close views of hand, notebook, and page. | A readable hand-and-page crop is available and naturally square-friendly. | Visually clear, but carries the film’s manipulative thriller context. |
| **The Shining** | Jack Torrance works at a typewriter, with pages feeding through the machine. | The typewriter and paper silhouette are distinctive even at icon scale. | High recognition, but it represents typing rather than handwriting and has a horror tone. |
| **Taxi Driver** | Travis Bickle keeps a written diary, with the film using his writing and narration as a core device. | A diary-and-pen crop can be intimate and monochrome. | Worth sourcing only if the darker character association is desired. |

## Evidence and source notes

The Lunchbox is explicitly described as a paper-based epistolary film in [Film Comment’s review](https://www.filmcomment.com/review-the-lunchbox-irrfan-khan-nimrat-kaur/). A search result for [The Prestige screenplay](https://imsdb.com/scripts/Prestige,-The.html) contains the direction that Angier opens a leather-bound journal and starts to write; it also describes Borden writing with paper and pencil. Search references for Joker, Gone Girl, and Taxi Driver confirm their characters’ journal or diary-writing scenes. The Shining’s typewriter-writing sequence is widely referred to as the *All Work and No Play* scene.

> A direct movie still may be copyrighted. Before making the public GitHub Pages icon final, prefer either a user-supplied still used with permission or an original icon illustration inspired by the selected scene’s composition. This avoids placing a studio still directly in a public repository while retaining the desired scene reference.

## Reading-scene direction

The owner changed the brief from writing to reading. The candidates below were checked against the Watchlist and are better aligned with the app’s core act of opening, reading, and learning from a book.

| Rank | Title and reading moment | Icon fit | Tone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **Breaking Bad**: Hank Schrader reads Walt’s copy of *Leaves of Grass* in the bathroom and discovers Gale’s inscription. | The close framing of Hank, the open book, and his realization reads clearly in a square crop. | Funny, recognisable, and a little unhinged in the best way. |
| **2** | **Game of Thrones**: Samwell Tarly studies at the Citadel library among chained books and high shelves. | The strongest literal-library visual and a very safe book-first silhouette. | Scholarly, epic, warm. |
| **3** | **The Prestige**: Angier reads Borden’s coded diary, while Borden reads Angier’s journal in prison. | Leather diary plus character profile can look refined in a tight crop. | Vintage, secretive, literary. |
| **4** | **Nocturnal Animals**: Susan Morrow reads the manuscript sent by her former husband. | An elegant close crop of hands, manuscript, and concentrated face is possible. | Premium, quiet, melancholic. |
| **5** | **The Lighthouse**: Winslow reads Wake’s logbook, which triggers the final conflict. | Strong monochrome contrast if a logbook close-up is sourced. | Strange, nautical, severe. |

### Confirmed scene evidence

An editorial recap in [Slate](https://slate.com/culture/2012/09/breaking-bad-review-hank-does-some-bathroom-reading.html) confirms that Hank finds Gale’s inscription to the “other favorite W.W.” while reading Walt’s *Leaves of Grass* in the bathroom. [TIME](https://time.com/4860875/game-of-thrones-samwell-tarly-citadel-research-clues/) describes Sam’s Citadel-library research, while a later library scene shows Sam studying. The [The Prestige screenplay](https://imsdb.com/scripts/Prestige,-The.html) describes Borden opening Angier’s journal to read, and Angier reading Borden’s notebook. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/film/2016/nov/03/nocturnal-animals-review-tom-ford-amy-adams-jake-gyllenhaal) describes Susan beginning to read the manuscript in *Nocturnal Animals*. Search evidence for *The Lighthouse* confirms that Winslow reads Wake’s critical logbook.
